The original continuity proof was mathematically valid. The final note uses
an elementary finite counting bound, which is stronger and requires no
continuity of the density-value function.

Mathematics checked:
1. Both counts range over natural numbers 2 <= n <= X, with real X >= 2.
2. A point in B but not A has P(n)=n^alpha. The positive-integer value
   P(n) determines n uniquely because alpha>0. There are at most floor(X^alpha).
3. A point in A but not B has n^beta<P(n+1)<(n+1)^beta. The intervals are
   disjoint for distinct integer n because beta>0. The values are positive
   integers below (X+1)^beta, giving the second floor bound.
4. The two exceptional-set counts bound the absolute count difference.
5. Normalization by X gives a quantity tending to zero for alpha,beta<1.
6. No existence of the target density is assumed in the argument.
7. The analytic joint-density law is an explicit premise. Three-variable
   independence and an independent proof of that law are not claimed.
